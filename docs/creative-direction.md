# Heather Newbuild Cottage: creative direction notes

Working notes for editing @heather_newbuild_cottage TikToks. The full brand
guide was supplied in conversation; this file records the essentials plus what
has been learned from reviewing real footage and feedback.

## Non-negotiables

- No stylised filters, grain, vignettes or lighting changes. A very gentle
  soft, warm grade is welcome if paint colours and wood tones stay true;
  avoid excess yellow, orange, contrast or saturation.
- No zooms, flashy transitions or effects added for the sake of looking edited.
- No cheesy, generic or "AI-sounding" text. Fewer words is better; no text
  is often best.
- Never state details that cannot be verified from footage (paint names,
  techniques, history of a piece). Ask instead.
- Analyse the footage before editing: best shots, textures, colours, light,
  story.
- Export 1080x1920 from the original clips, never from a TikTok download
  (compressed, and any burned-in text cannot be removed).

## Taste, as seen in the footage

- Light: low, raking golden sunlight across wood grain is the most beautiful
  light in the home. Shots with it deserve the most screen time.
- Textures: bare pine grain, sawdust, brush bristles, turned legs, painted
  spindles.
- Colours that sing: warm putty/cream paint, honey pine, the dark heritage
  green kitchen cabinets, dark wood floor. Bright yellow sugar soap and red
  heart sponges are branded props: show them briefly, don't linger.
- Story shape that works: cold grey-painted pine -> stripped back -> warm
  putty and honey pine. The colour change itself is the story.
- Hands at work (painting, brushing, sanding) feel authentic and personal.
- A second, smaller reveal (e.g. a matching chair) makes a satisfying end.

## Text overlays

- House style: a magazine lockup set with libass. Lead words in small spaced
  capitals (Cormorant Garamond SemiBold, ~56px, wide tracking), the phrase
  below in Cormorant Garamond Medium Italic (~86px). Ivory with a soft
  blurred edge; gentle fades. Placed clear of the subject and TikTok UI.
- Rejected typography: Cormorant Medium upright ("very poor", spindly);
  large SemiBold Italic sentences ("still poor", wedding-stationery feel,
  dominated the frame); any hard offset drop shadow.
- Prefer useful, quiet text (e.g. the paint colour on the painting shot)
  over slogans.
- Rejected: "a soft, chalky trim paint" (inaccurate), "and here she is...
  ready for tea & cake" (cheesy).

## Edit history

- Table upcycle v1: vintage grade + four captions. Rejected: filter and
  lighting change.
- Table upcycle v2: natural colour, hard cuts, one dissolve, two overlays.
- Table upcycle v3 (refinement of v2): Cormorant Garamond, gentle soft/warm
  grade (warmth only on cool-white shots), short dissolves through the
  process, reveal given about half the running time. Overlays kept:
  "Back to bare pine", "A little farmhouse charm / for a new build".
- Table upcycle v4: added the tabletop tracking shot (IMG_9094) and the chair
  (IMG_9097, reversed so it settles on the chair). Reveal is now ~15s of
  ~24s and ends resting on the chair. Those two shots get a touch of
  neutralising instead of warmth: bright window light turned the pine salmon.
- Table upcycle v5: fixed "incredibly bright" footage. Root cause: HDR was
  converted with a 100-nit reference instead of the 203-nit HLG standard,
  doubling brightness; the shadow lift washed it out further. Now 203 nits
  with hable tone mapping, no lifted blacks, warmth on all reveal shots and
  ivory text over the blinds. Always check exposure against the phone.
- Table upcycle v6: overlays switched to Cormorant Garamond SemiBold Italic,
  ivory, soft blurred shadow.
- Table upcycle v7: captions reset as magazine lockups ("BACK TO / bare
  pine", "A LITTLE FARMHOUSE CHARM / for a new build").
- Frenchic collaborations: products clearly visible in the edit, but never
  framed like an advert.
